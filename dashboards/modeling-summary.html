Distribution and scaling

Market Score is approximately normal, so no target transformation is applied for simplicity. Lasso applies StandardScaler to numerical features because its penalty depends on coefficient scale. Baseline OLS remains in original units because scaling is not required for fit and original units improve interpretation. Categorical variables are one-hot encoded, not scaled.

Historical Stability

This check tests whether period-to-period scores remain reasonably stable when the fixed latest-period models are applied across the complete history. It measures annual absolute changes, the share of ZIPs with large observation-to-observation jumps, maximum jumps, and the frequency of scores clipped to 0 or 100. Because observations are irregular, annual changes use each ZIP's final observation in consecutive calendar years; adjacent-record changes use the available observations and therefore may span unequal time intervals.

2. Modeling methodology

OLS benchmark
3 variables

Brute-force OLS over valid 2–4 variable specifications. The small search space favors explanation, reviewability, and direct business interpretation.

LassoCV
12 variables

Applies incompatibility rules first, then uses five-fold cross-validation to tune shrinkage and reduce complexity while preserving predictive information.

Champion: refitted LassoCV
9 variables

Removes two economically inconsistent variables and provides the best balance of fit, explanation, and business checks.

Why both methods?

Brute-force OLS deliberately limits original variables to 2–4. This makes the benchmark easy to explain and allows conventional diagnostics to remain readable. LassoCV starts from a broader compatible set and shrinks weak coefficients to zero, offering a lower-complexity alternative to an unrestricted regression. The refit is a business-sign contingency, not a new validation exercise.

How does Lasso handle missing predictors?

The Lasso pipeline includes imputation inside its preprocessing step. Numerical missing values are replaced with the training-sample median before standardization, while missing categorical values are replaced with the training-sample mode before one-hot encoding. During cross-validation, these replacement values are learned separately from each training fold and then applied to its held-out fold, which avoids using validation information during preprocessing. The fitted pipeline carries the same learned rules into latest-period and full-period scoring; unseen category levels are safely ignored. Although the 83-row latest-period calibration sample has no missing cells, this preprocessing allows the fitted model to score historical or application rows whose selected predictors are incomplete. Imputation supplies a usable input—it does not create new market information—so outputs based on imputed predictors should still be monitored.

Selection criteria

  • Statistical: RMSE, MAE, R², adjusted R², Spearman rank correlation, variable t-tests or categorical joint F-tests, overall F-test, AIC/AICc/BIC, VIF, condition number, and Cook’s distance.
  • Business: expected coefficient direction; coverage of price, demand/supply, and market-speed families; and exclusion of competing level/MOM/YOY versions of the same measure.
  • Validation: each corrected row is a unique ZIP, so shuffled five-fold validation produces out-of-fold Lasso predictions. These metrics are the best current estimate of generalization.

How did you constrain predictions to 0–100?

The OLS and refitted-Lasso models first generate unrestricted continuous predictions. Each prediction is then rounded to the nearest integer and passed through a deterministic score-range rule: values below 0 are floored at 0, values above 100 are capped at 100, and values already within the range are retained. The notebook counts predictions outside each boundary before clipping and prints a message whenever either rule is triggered. The champion’s constrained integer output is stored as Market_Score_hat_latest. This is a post-model reporting constraint; it does not alter model fitting or coefficients.

4. Latest-period calibration and reference validation

How was the model fitted to the reference scores?

For every ZIP with a 2025 observation, the calibration date is its maximum PERIOD_END within 2025. The reference table is joined by ZIP, leaving 83 unique labeled latest-period rows with no missing model fields. Baseline OLS and LassoCV are fitted only on these aligned rows; earlier observations are not used as additional training labels. Lasso preprocessing median-imputes numerical predictors if needed, one-hot encodes nominal categories, and standardizes numerical inputs before penalization.

The initial Lasso applies feature-incompatibility rules and selects its penalty by five-fold cross-validation. Two selected variables fail their expected economic signs—MEDIAN_PPSF_MOM and INVENTORY_MOM—so the contingency refit removes them and reruns LassoCV on the 83 calibration rows. The resulting nine-variable refitted Lasso is the champion.

How does the model convert market metrics into a score?

The model standardizes each numerical input, multiplies it by its fitted coefficient, and adds the contributions with the intercept. Faster and more competitive conditions raise the score: more homes sold above list and more homes going off market within two weeks contribute positively, while longer median days on market contributes negatively. The continuous result is rounded to an integer and clipped to the required 0–100 range.

9. Full-period Scoring (Adjusted)

This contingency tests whether limiting historical predictor extrapolation and aligning the score distribution with the latest-period calibration regime can improve stability. It does not retrain the champion model.

Stage 1 — Reference-based predictor trimming

For each of the nine numerical champion predictors, Tukey limits are estimated from the 83 latest-period reference rows: Q1 − 1.5×IQR and Q3 + 1.5×IQR. Historical values outside those limits are floored or capped, missing values remain available to the fitted pipeline's imputer, and the unchanged refitted-LassoCV pipeline scores all 12,445 rows. The most frequently adjusted variable is OFF_MARKET_IN_TWO_WEEKS (2,364 rows; 19.0%), followed by MEDIAN_LIST_PPSF_MOM (10.8%) and MEDIAN_SALE_PRICE_MOM (10.5%).

Stage 2 — Fitted-Normal distribution overlay

Consider:

  • A: latest-period data
  • B: full-period data

Transform B → A

Rather than directly mapping B to the empirical distribution of A, assume that
the latest-period data follows a Normal distribution:

YA ∼ 𝒩(μA,σA2),

where μA and σA are estimated from the latest-period sample A.

For the full-period data:

YB ∼ FB.

First, map each observation in B to its percentile under the distribution of B:

UB = FB(YB).

Then map this percentile to the fitted Normal distribution of A:

Y′B
=
F−1A,Normal(UB)
=
μA + σA Φ−1(UB).

Therefore,

Y′B = μA + σA Φ−1(FB(YB))

where Φ−1 is the inverse CDF (quantile function) of the standard
Normal distribution.

The transformed variable Y′B approximately follows:

Y′B ∼ 𝒩(μA,σA2).

The IQR-trimmed full-period score is converted to its empirical midpoint percentile, U=(rank−0.5)/n, then mapped through a Normal distribution fitted to the latest-period reference scores: Y′=μA+σAΦ⁻¹(U). The fitted reference parameters are μ=61.36 and σ=13.50. Mapped values are rounded to integers and constrained to 0–100. This preserves rank ordering while aligning the full-period marginal score distribution with a smooth latest-period target.

Interpretation and contingencies

  • The overlay adjusts the marginal score distribution; it does not prove that historical reference scores would have followed the latest-period distribution.
  • Forcing history toward the latest-period regime may suppress genuine housing cycles, so the adjusted output should remain a sensitivity or contingency result until back-testing is available.
  • An alternative is to adjust selected explanatory variables where the change is economically defensible. Independent mapping can distort relationships among predictors; multivariate or copula-based methods could preserve the latest-period dependence structure.
  • Any production decision should compare final-score stability, ZIP ranking, calibration preservation, driver realism, and sensitivity to the chosen reference window.

The final mapped output is saved as scores_all_periods_adjusted.csv with columns Zip Code, City, County, PERIOD, and MarketScore_hat.

Interpretation and current decision

The selected linear champion is the refitted LassoCV. Among models that pass the business-direction checks, it offers the best overall statistical performance, manageable complexity, and clear feature-family coverage. It retains nine original variables, materially improves in-sample fit over the OLS benchmark, and passes all defined business sign checks.

Full-period treatments: the unadjusted champion remains the transparent primary scoring method. Two contingency treatments were also developed for historical application: first, cap or floor the nine numerical model predictors using 1.5×IQR limits estimated from the latest-period reference sample and score them with the unchanged champion pipeline; second, map the resulting score percentiles to a Normal distribution fitted to the latest-period scores. Predictor trimming materially improves the pre-overlay stability diagnostics, while the fitted-Normal overlay aligns the final marginal score distribution with the latest-period regime. Because the overlay may suppress genuine market cycles and final post-overlay stability has not yet been recalculated, scores_all_periods_adjusted.csv should be treated as a sensitivity or contingency output rather than replacing the unadjusted score.

The champion refit has not yet received a new outer five-fold CV evaluation; that validation should precede production use.
